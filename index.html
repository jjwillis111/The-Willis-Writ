About

I am a law student at Boston University School of Law, originally from Tonganoxie, Kansas, with roots in the Kansas City area.

My interest in the law began on a freshman-year trip to Germany. The bullet holes still visible on the Reichstag building were a vivid reminder that the rule of law is something people build, and something that has to be defended.

Since then I have worked in a prosecutor's office, on a U.S. Senate investigative subcommittee, in the legal and business affairs group of a Major League Soccer club, and this fall in the Division of Enforcement at the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

Focus

My work so far falls into three areas.

  1. Investigations and enforcement. A district attorney's office, the Senate's Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, and the SEC's Division of Enforcement.
  2. Privacy, health, and media law. Transactional clinic work for clients in each of these fields.
  3. Commercial and international matters. Discovery and contract review in-house, and empirical research on investor-state dispute settlement reform.

Volume V

Research

Empirical work on dispute settlement reform

UNCITRAL Working Group III

Research Assistant to Professor Weijia Rao, with co-author Niccolò Ridi · Summer 2026

An empirical study of how states have positioned themselves in the Working Group's deliberations on reform of investor-state dispute settlement (ISDS).

I coded delegates' statements from the Working Group's sessions, matched them to the reform propositions in the study, flagged expressions of support, and grouped the remaining claims by theme.

Method

The coding workbook, built in Python, has four sheets.

  1. The reform propositions from the report.
  2. The claims made at each meeting, quoted verbatim.
  3. A match of each claim to a proposition.
  4. A thematic clustering of the claims that match no proposition.

What it taught me

Working with Professor Rao, I helped develop a strict coding protocol: quote the source verbatim, re-audit line by line, and never trust a partial read of a transcript.

That habit of disciplined source work carries directly into legal research and into any investigation where the record has to hold up.
