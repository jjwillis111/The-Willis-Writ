The Willis Writ

A daily legal newspaper, behind the globe on the shelf

The Willis Writ gathers headlines from public sources on regulatory and policy developments where law, technology and public policy meet, and sorts them by subject.

It reads the way a paper reads. The most recent story leads the front page, shorter stories run in columns beneath it, and each beat has its own section.

Open The Willis Writ

The five sections

  1. SEC. Press releases and speeches from the Commission.
  2. Privacy. FTC notices, California's privacy agency, and law-firm alerts.
  3. Regulations. Proposed and final rules from the Federal Register, beginning with the SEC and the FTC.
  4. Congress. Bills sent to the President or signed into law, from Congress.gov and GovTrack.
  5. Supreme Court. Coverage of the Court from SCOTUSblog.

How it works

  1. Each morning an automated job collects the latest items from every source.
  2. Each source is fetched on its own. If one fails, its last good stories stay in the paper, so a single broken feed never leaves a section empty.
  3. The paper carries headlines and links only. Nothing is rewritten or summarized, and every story opens at its source.
  4. The site stays a single static page. The paper reads one small data file, and the daily job is what updates it.
